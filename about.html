I'm a young software developer from Northern Ireland. I started building websites because I was the kid in school who'd rather pull apart a laptop than play football, and somewhere along the way it turned into a job.

These days I build websites and custom software for small and medium businesses — the kind of places where the owner still knows everyone's name. Family-run guesthouses. Independent retailers. White-goods businesses. Tradespeople with vans and a few staff and a real need for software that just works.

The reason is simple. Big agencies don't really want to work with small businesses — there's no money in it for them. And the cheap end of the market is full of pretty templates that fall over after a year. There's a big middle ground where most local businesses live, and they keep getting badly served. So I'm trying to do something about it.

What that means in practice

  • Honest pricing — no surprise invoices, no padding
  • You talk to me, not a project manager and three juniors
  • Real code I'd be happy to show you under the bonnet
  • Built to last — not abandoned the day you pay the final invoice
  • Support that doesn't come from a different time zone

How I work

The first conversation is free, and it's a conversation — not a sales pitch. You tell me what the business is doing and what's getting in the way. I tell you honestly whether I think I can help, what it'd cost, and how long it'd take. If I'm not the right person for it, I'll say so and try to point you somewhere better.

Once we're working together, you get a single point of contact — me — and a build that's done properly. When it goes live, I don't disappear. Sites need looking after. Software needs looking after. So do the people running the business.
